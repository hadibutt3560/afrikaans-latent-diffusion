## Question and controls

Does decoder dropout improve held-out reconstruction compared with the otherwise identical full-decoder adaptation? Job74177's F12 development CE reached 0.978 at step4920 and rose to1.337 at its selected step26520 while logged training losses declined. This suggests overfitting, not a demonstrated decoder-only cause or proof of irreversible compression loss. The older dropout comparison used32 training examples and altered backbone dropout; it is not held-out evidence against this experiment.

D0: all12 mT5 decoder blocks plus final layer norm, dropout disabled. D1: identical trainable parameters with existing decoder dropout0.1 active during training. Both begin from original AE job56531/step30500 SHA256 `123f4508e4aa26967c05a2ad413cebb8f2a2b90fc2420dab757704495dc27f5c`, fresh AdamW. Never start from F12-best. All evaluation uses dropout off. The mT5 encoder, entire Perceiver, embeddings, output projection and diffusion stay frozen; latent caches do not change. Original training corpus47767 passages, tokenizer and128-token view; cached32x64 latents of norm8. No test inputs in the package.

Token-weighted teacher-forced CE, FP32, TF32 off; AdamW2e-5, betas0.9/0.999, decay0.01,100-update warmup then constant; clip1; microbatch1,accumulation8. Same complete deterministic permutations seeded20261024+epoch, retaining tails. Each arm starts from RNG20261024, then preserves its own RNG and optimizer state. Decoder dropout is the only treatment difference. Inference beam4, max128/min5, repetition penalty1.2, no-repeat token trigram3, length penalty1, deterministic.
