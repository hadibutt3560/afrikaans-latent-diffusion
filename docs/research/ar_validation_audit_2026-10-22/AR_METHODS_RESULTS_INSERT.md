## Methods insertion

We conducted a validation-only audit of the historical randomly initialised autoregressive model, using its previously selected step 60,000 checkpoint. The exported weights were compared tensor-for-tensor with the selected checkpoint, and the saved tokenizer was compared with the frozen diffusion tokenizer on all validation passages. We reproduced the original 128-example validation subset by applying the recorded seed 42 shuffle. The checkpoint was then evaluated on the full 5,905-example validation partition without reselection. Cross-entropy was aggregated by scored-token count, including the original EOS treatment, with the extra BOS and padding positions excluded from loss. Uncertainty for full-validation cross-entropy was estimated by resampling the 251 source documents in 2,000 percentile bootstrap replicates.

For unconditional generation, we specified two inference arms before fresh sampling. Each contained256 outputs with generation seeds30000–30255, nucleus sampling at top-p 0.95, top-k 0 and temperature 1, no repetition constraints, FP32 and a single BOS input. The primary arm allowed127 new tokens; the historical-cap control allowed64. The larger allowance matched the diffusion decoder's maximum total position count while retaining different decoding algorithms. The two AR arms differed only in this cap. External scoring used the preserved mGPT implementation and pinned revision, with FP32, a512-token context and 256-token stride. Four stored validation-score anchors reproduced exactly. The paired length-cap comparison used2,000 generation-seed bootstrap replicates; these describe sample variability conditional on one trained AR model.

For context, the first256 outputs from each of the five stored diffusion validation banks per configuration were summarised using the same external scorer and whitespace-based diversity definitions. All training seeds were retained. Differences in pretraining, training exposure, compute, checkpoint selection, decoding and output length precluded interpretation as a prospectively matched architecture comparison. Human evaluation was not performed; the fixed 20-output inspection was explicitly AI-assisted.

## Results insertion

The AR export passed the weight and tokenizer checks. The reproduced selection-subset cross-entropy was 3.607105, compared with the stored3.607138. Full-validation cross-entropy was 3.594901, with document-bootstrap95% interval [3.2612,3.9712]. This supports the technical validity of the selected checkpoint; its causal perplexity of 36.412 is a different quantity from external perplexity assigned to generated text.

Increasing the generation allowance removed nontermination at the 64-token cap for 26 of 256 outputs. All256 paired sequences shared identical prefixes, and 230 decoded outputs were unchanged. External mGPT perplexity decreased from 85.6705 to 82.1786, with paired conditional95% interval [−5.3554,−1.5754] for the difference. The primary-arm sample-bootstrap interval was[74.7340,90.9093]. None of its outputs was empty or reached the larger cap, but11 of 256 were exact normalised matches to training text. The AI-assisted inspection identified both recognisable administrative forms and incomplete or semantically unstable passages. Mechanical termination therefore did not establish linguistic adequacy.

In equal-size validation generation subsets, small and large diffusion models had mean external perplexities53.0745±1.0484 and 50.0238±0.4386 across five training seeds. The corresponding descriptive ratios to the single AR bank were 0.64584 and 0.60872. These values are numerically below the proposal's1.20 threshold but do not establish that its matched comparison or accompanying quality conditions were satisfied. AR outputs averaged17.53 words, compared with 27.85 and 24.81 for the two diffusion configurations. Coarse length stratification also showed higher small-diffusion perplexity than AR in the two shortest bands, despite the aggregate ordering. The evidence supports a contextual automatic-metric comparison with explicit limitations, not a claim of linguistic or architectural superiority.
